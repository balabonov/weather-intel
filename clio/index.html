&&

.DISCUSSION...

Very warm today with a record high already broken at FNT, based on a 
20Z obs of 85F, but even warmer readings exist further northwest as 
IKW, MBS, and HYX log readings of 88F, 86F, and 86F, respectively.

An active period of weather gets underway this week as the upper 
level jet approaches the Great Lakes and then fixates through the 
workweek. At the surface, a chain of surface low pressures is 
anchored to a stationary front extending from the High Plains to the 
Upper Midwest. A shortwave trough will eject from a deep upper low 
stuck over Nevada tonight to establish a primary surface wave that 
will reach Lower Michigan on Tuesday. Meanwhile, a 594 dam 500 mb 
ridge of high pressure situated over the Gulf Coast supports 
continued anticyclonic warm/moist advection into the region. 
Moisture metrics are already on the rise this afternoon with most 
ASOS/AWOS sites reporting 60+ dewpoints. This ensures a mild night 
with overnight lows in the mid (perhaps upper) 60s, or about 20F 
above climatological normals. Before the low makes any progress into 
northern Lower, isentropic upsloping produces an uptick in mid 
column saturation (700-500 mb) while drier air resides within an 
elevated warm layer (925-800 mb). Scattered overnight showers will 
partially fill in overnight, and remain elevated. Forecast soundings 
show some elevated instability, maximizing at around 200 J/kg, so 
some rumbles of thunder cannot be ruled out. No severe weather 
concerns given the lack of CAPE density for severe hail and near-
surface stability to dampen the wind gust response.

CAMs show conditions drying out pretty quickly Tuesday morning given 
how quickly low and mid level moisture is stripped out, after flow 
veers WNW. The main forecast consideration will be temperature 
trends for the rest of the day with 925 mb temperatures starting out 
in the low 20s (Celsius). Skies should also clear out for the peak 
diurnal heating cycle, adding to the insolation potential. Models 
differ in timing the arrival of the primary cold front, but the more 
delayed the boundary is, the higher the possible max temperature 
will be. The latest NBM 5th percentile has 80s across most of Metro 
Detroit, so NWP confidence is high for another day with highs of 
80F. The record high for DTW on October 13 is within reach at 83F in 
1975. Seasonably mild Tuesday night behind the front, although still 
a 20+ degree nocturnal drop, even with thickening cloud cover.

The cold front looks to stall out near the Michigan/Ohio border 
Tuesday night and Wednesday as the jet axis approaches aloft. 
Numerous shortwave interactions promote a perturbed geopotential 
height field offering additional chances for precipitation. This 
will be augmented by the Gulf ridge which will still be in place, 
renewing theta-e advection. Limited CAPE, but non-zero leaves a 
chance for a rumble of thunder. Any shower activity that continues 
into the daylight hours will be brief as shortwave ridging builds in 
from the west, and flow flips NNE, dislodging the boundary. This 
also allows a more seasonable airmass to filter in from Ontario. 
Cold advection persists into Thursday, before the next wave of low 
pressure lifts into southern Lower Thursday evening or during the 
overnight hours. This brings another round of rain and a cold front. 
Cooler and drier conditions develop Friday, lasting through the 
weekend.

&&

.MARINE...

Low pressure will travel from the Plains into northern lower 
Michigan tonight through tomorrow morning. This will quickly 
strengthen the pressure gradient ahead of the arrival of this 
system, increasing the magnitude of southwest flow across southern 
Lake Huron, Lake St. Clair, and Lake Erie. More tempered northerly 
flow will be found across northern Lake Huron through the morning. 
As low pressure progresses east into New England, wind direction 
will back more westerly through the afternoon, which will bring a 
window for some breezy conditions across the southern Great Lakes. 
Wind gusts around 20 to 25 knots will be possible, with wind speeds 
quickly diminishing through the evening as the pressure gradient 
weakens. Rain showers with some isolated thunderstorms will be 
likely through tomorrow. 

A cold front will then push through the Great Lakes Wednesday, 
stalling across southern Michigan by the afternoon. The favorable 
northwest fetch will produce wind gusts 20 to 30 knots across 
northern Lake Huron. High pressure to then build in through Thursday 
which will bring a period of light winds for the day. Confidence is 
increasing for a strong cold front to pass Friday into Saturday. 
Instability will increase over the lakes in the wake of the front 
given the advent of cold air over the warmer lake waters. Coupled 
with longer duration northwest flow, a longer duration gale event is 
looking increasingly likely to end the week.
