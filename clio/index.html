The synoptic environment continues to evolve as expected given the highly amplified omega block draped across CONUS. Southeast Michigan remains positioned near the eastern longwave trough axis which will undergo a restructuring during the course of the day. The primary upper low is expected to become absorbed by a retrograding 528 dam 500 mb low tracking southwestward along the St. Lawrence River, which then settles over the Northeast this evening. Meanwhile, upstream longwave ridging drifts eastward, easing into the Upper Midwest/Minnesota. This results in the local area becoming positioned between the complementary waves while an embedded speed max transits due south over the central Great Lakes. The CVA induces subtle forcing aloft to maintain localized areas of lake effect precipitation for most of the day. However, coverage will be limited to the eastern Thumb and locations along the immediate southern Lake Huron shoreline, after northerly flow backs north-northwesterly later today. Thermodynamically, deterministic solutions indicate slight degradation of the existing thermal trough as weak warm advection slips in from the encroaching ridge. 850 mb temperatures should lift by 2 or 3 C today, but still anomalously cold, resulting in highs only in the mid 40s. Regarding precipitation type within the narrow corridor of lake effect showers, forecast soundings reveal eventual drying through the DGZ as flow backs between 5 and 7 kft AGL. This limits the likelihood for ice nuclei, therefore, rain is the preferred hydrometeor form as the day progresses. Note that some snowflakes could still mix in during the morning hours today given deeper saturation depths. Not as chilly overnight thanks to insulating lake stratocumulus, but still colder than normal. The lower tropospheric anticyclone builds northeastward out of the Mid-Mississippi Valley Sunday, weakening boundary layer flow. Lower column winds now look to retain a northwesterly, if not north- northwesterly trajectory well into the afternoon hours, but the lack of shear should suffice in concluding residual lake effect precipitation by mid afternoon. Significant column drying and subsidence ensue Sunday night as the longwave ridge crosses overhead. 1030 mb surface high pressure gradually builds into the Ohio Valley with time which flips weak gradient flow southwesterly. Temperatures moderate throughout next week as the ridge flattens but lingers from southern Appalachia into the Southeast.
