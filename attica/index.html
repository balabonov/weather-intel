Ridging aloft and at the surface continue to dominate the region and will provide several more days of quiet weather around the Great Lakes. The rex block will remain mostly intact the next couple days before slowly breaking down as the book end troughs break down in favor of a more zonal pattern by the middle of next week. The last few night fog has been the main concern in the forecast as is usually the case with ridging and flow off the Great Lakes this time of year. Ample moisture with longer cool nights brings areas of fog across the state, heavily influenced by wind direct deciding which locations get the fog and how dense. As the center of the surface drifts southward from southern Quebec toward the east coast of the conus, the surface flow will change from northeasterly to southeasterly today as we become positioned on the west side of the high. The change in flow will lead to the fog likely shifting from the Thumb and Saginaw Valley down to the state line with the flow coming off Lake Erie. Will keep the entry level patchy fog mention in for now and look more at it this afternoon. The upper level ridge axis will cross the area Saturday night. This will increase the southwesterly flow into the region allowing for a boost in temps Sunday. After slowly moderating upward through the mid 60s to end the week, Sunday could support highs in the 70s for at least part of the area. Looking into next week, a developing low tracking across Canada looks to send a weak backdoor cold front into the region. The airmass is very dry with ridging across the SW conus blocking any moisture return, the front will offer some cooler temps as 850mb temps fall from the upper single digits C, down to just above 0C behind the front with the next surface ridge. && .MARINE...
