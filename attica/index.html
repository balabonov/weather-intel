No significant weather expected today while a jet max ejects across The UP and into Ontario. The occluded low pressure system stemming from this wave exhibits vertical continuity through the 300 mb level while the progressive surface reflection tracks from The Northwest Angle to James Bay between 12Z and 00Z today. Meanwhile, lower tropospheric theta-e advection begins to work into Southeast Michigan from the confluent southwesterly trajectories. The latest NCEP solutions continue to resolve nil QPF given the lack of precipitation response upon arrival of the system's cold front. The front is still expected to transition to a stationary boundary over Lower Michigan, and will be met with ambient dryness through much of the atmospheric column. A representative sampling of area forecast soundings indicates broad dewpoint depressions and established static stability between the 5 kft and 15 kft AGL layers, attributed to the distant influence of Mid-Atlantic mid level ridging. PWATs will rise, marked by the present increase in high cloud fraction and an attempt later today at some component of diurnal cumuliform cloud. Compared to the 19.00Z KDTX RAOB, PWATs increase by a factor of three this afternoon, from 0.22 to around 0.60 inches. However, the lack of saturation precludes any potential for rain. Main forecast concern today remains focused on southwesterly wind magnitudes as the CWA becomes positioned between strengthening gradient flow from the low to the northwest and surface high pressure to the southeast. As discussed over the last several forecast cycles, the shallow mixing depths will limit the frequency and duration of peak gusts today. The strongest low level jet winds should reside farther north with closer proximity to slightly cooler air. Areas north of M-46 stand the best chance to see gusts of 30+ mph this afternoon while gusts should generally hold below 25 mph toward the south. Ridging returns Friday with dry weather persisting into the weekend. Any westerly component to area winds drops off as the day progresses, backing southerly by the afternoon hours as the surface anticyclone recenters over central Appalachia. Upstream, a Pacific longwave trough works through the Four Corners area before gradually phasing with a meandering upper low moving south across the Canadian Prairies. As this unfolds, the eastern CONUS jet will strengthen, although split-flow should reside over/near Southeast Michigan into Saturday. Double digit 850 mb temperatures are becoming more likely on Saturday resulting in widespread highs within the low to mid 60s. The next chance for rain comes Sunday as the composite trough axis releases across the Midwest. Latest medium range solutions have shown signs of deamplification upon reaching the Great Lakes, but the baroclinic zone and kinematics favor a period of showers on Sunday.
