NOTE
An cold front is in the process of clearing SE MI and will bring about breezy conditions, steadily falling temperatures, and periodic rain showers for today. The departure of low pressure from Michigan into Ontario and Quebec will have cold air advection ramp up through the day as low level flow backs from west to north-northwest. The advection of cooler air will greatly dampen or nullify the typical diurnal peak in temperatures this afternoon as they fall from the 50s-60s this morning into the 40s by the evening. This will be reinforced from a secondary cold front that will progress across SE MI late this morning into the early afternoon hours. Passage of this secondary frontal boundary will further increase mixing depths, producing breezy conditions with gusts ranging between 30-35 mph. Some isolated potential to 40 mph will be possible after the passage of the secondary cold front, but the absence of appreciable stronger wind speeds within the mixed layer lowers confidence for higher frequency gusts to 40+ mph. The exception will be within the Thumb given reduced surface friction along the lake as flow backs to the NNW. Retainment of appreciable low-level moisture wrapped in cyclonic flow coupled with shallow instability within the mixed layer will support drizzle and rain shower chances through the afternoon and evening hours. Coverage of rain showers will start to decrease in the evening hours for most locations as flow backs to the north, which cuts off the additive moisture advection derived from Lake Michigan. Consequently, coverage will likely increase around the Lake Huron shoreline once wind direction orients more favorably off of Lake Huron. The enhanced pocket of shallow instability coming off the lake will supplement moisture advection onshore. Notably stronger lapse rates extending through 700mb and positive buoyancy extending through the -10C isotherm could support more vigorous convective shower development and/or isolated thunderstorms, capable of producing small hail. Strong high pressure will rapidly build in from west to east overnight which will scour and advect out low-level stratus as dry air works in. Clear skies coupled with the early day cold air advection will bring chilly overnight lows. Efficient radiational cooling drop temperatures into the low to mid 30s, bringing high potential for patchy to widespread frost. The coldest temperatures will be found across the interior of SE MI through the Tri-Cities where temperatures in the upper 20s to low 30s are possible. Below normal temperatures hold tomorrow afternoon with highs capped in the low to mid 50s. High pressure then sets up and expands over the Midwest and Great Lakes next week which will bring an extended period of dry weather. Temperatures rebound back to above normal values in the mid to upper 60s, possibly low 70s, by the late week period.
