NOTE
Yesterday's high temperatures once again reached into the mid 60s across most areas, as the 00Z DTX sounding checked in with a 925 mb temp of 11 C. Temperatures should be even warmer today (65 to 70 degrees) as 925 mb temps are advertised to reach near 13 C. Thus, there is an excellent chance Flint ties or breaks its daily record high for Nov 21. A weaker pressure gradient over eastern areas will allow surface flow backed a bit more out of the south-southeast to exert some cooler marine influence and thus result in the lower values. An upper-level wave and cold pool sinking south into Montana today will team up with a strong PV anomaly descending south out of the Arctic Circle and sliding south along the Manitoba/Ontario border on Sunday. This will lead to much colder temperatures next week. One more warm day is expected on Sunday, with temperatures likely rising back into the low to mid 60s as a cold front and rain showers arrive late in the day into early Sunday evening. With the main low and height falls tracking north of Lower Michigan, indications are that the line of showers along the frontal boundary will be in a weakening state as it crosses Southeast Michigan, and QPF amounts will likely be under a tenth of an inch, with some locations struggling to even measure. Aggressive drying arrives by midnight as 700 mb dewpoint depressions increase to 30 C by Monday morning. Nonetheless, a strong upper- level wave will swing through northern Lower Michigan Monday evening and send 850 mb temps into the -9 to -12 C range. There is an increasing chance (supported by the Euro model) that the northern Thumb region will see light accumulating snow, as there is even synoptic support from the narrow, short-lived forcing along the Arctic boundary tracking through Monday evening. A 700 mb cold pool (-20C) will track across Lake Huron Monday night, with warming and an upper-level ridge quickly building in on Tuesday, putting an end to any potential lake-effect activity. The low-level air mass will remain chilly as 850 mb temps are progged to hold in the negative mid single digits, resulting in maxes holding in the low to mid 40s for the midweek period. && .MARINE... High pressure extending from the Great Lakes to east coast will provide light winds and mostly clear skies to start the weekend. Ridge of high pressure is also present in the upper levels across the eastern conus while a deep trough exits across the western conus. This all leads to a period of southerly flow and a warm stable airmass over the region at least to start the weekend. As the trough pushes eastward, a surface low will lift northeast through the Midwest setting up a cold front that will pass through the region Sunday evening into the overnight. This will open the door for stronger northwesterly flow and cold air advection pushing across the lakes. At this point the winds look to stay at or below 30 knots, but there will be potential for at least Small Craft Advisories Sunday night into the new week with the increasing winds and building wave heights. && .CLIMATE... .DISCUSSION...

Yesterday's high temperatures once again reached into the mid 60s 
across most areas, as the 00Z DTX sounding checked in with a 925 mb

high for Nov 21. A weaker pressure gradient over eastern areas will 
allow surface flow backed a bit more out of the south-southeast to 
exert some cooler marine influence and thus result in the lower 
values.   

An upper-level wave and cold pool sinking south into Montana today 
will team up with a strong PV anomaly descending south out of the 
Arctic Circle and sliding south along the Manitoba/Ontario border on 
Sunday. This will lead to much colder temperatures next week. One 
more warm day is expected on Sunday, with temperatures likely rising 
back into the low to mid 60s as a cold front and rain showers arrive 
late in the day into early Sunday evening. With the main low and

weakening state as it crosses Southeast Michigan, and QPF amounts
