Surface low pressure is centered over the Ohio Valley this morning with a trailing hybrid inverted trough/cold front draped northwestward across the Thumb into northern Lower MI. As the low shifts east today, the trough will swing southward across SE MI with synoptic support for ascent from an amplified mid-level shortwave passing overhead. Scattered to numerous rain showers accompany the trough with gusty north to northeast wind (~25-30 mph) ensuing behind it. It will be a rather raw day as temperatures reach the mid 40s but the wind keeps the wind chill near or below 40 F. Cold air advection gains traction tonight with 850 mb temps falling to around -6 C by Friday morning. This will be the driver behind an early lake effect precip event tonight into the late week. Showers will initially be cold rain but transition to mainly melting snow overnight. Lake effect showers quickly become organized within the cold NNE flow over Lake Huron this evening, with models continuing to advertise two dominant convergent bands - one in the western/central Thumb and one along the eastern shoreline of the Thumb into the Port Huron vicinity. These will be fueled by long fetch over near record warm Lake Huron temps still in the mid 50s F, resulting in sfc-850mb delta-T of nearly 20 C. This contributes to impressive lake induced CAPE of 400 to 700 J/kg with the modified sounding showing convective depth exceeding 10 kft and favorable intersection with the DGZ, indicating efficient snowflake production and high precip rates overnight into midday Friday. The warmth of the lake will end up being a mitigating factor for snow accumulation, especially along the lakeshore where wet bulb temps struggle to fall below the mid 30s. A few miles inland however, the ambient temperature and dew points tenuously near freezing will allow for minor slushy accumulations throughout portions of the Thumb Friday morning. Latest ensemble output continues to target areas downwind of the dominant band in eastern Sanilac and St. Clair Counties for localized amounts up to 1 to 2". Low confidence exists for diabatic cooling within the most vigorous precip rates to lower column temps by a few degrees, leaving the door open for local overachievement in this area. Daytime Friday will be brisk with wind chill holding in the 30s. Subtle backing to wind direction causes the lake effect bands to shift mainly offshore by late Friday afternoon. However, the immediate eastern shoreline of the Thumb remains a target for additional rain/snow into Friday night. Elsewhere, dry and cold conditions carry temperatures down into the 20s by early Saturday morning. Cool cyclonic flow holds through Saturday but as the thermal trough begins to ease out, any remaining vigor to lake effect ends and temperatures rebound toward the mid 40s. A highly amplified ridge then builds into the Great Lakes Sunday into Monday, offering dry and stable conditions.
